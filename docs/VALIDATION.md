# Talking to real users

The goal is to find out whether anyone has the problem LGtie solves and would use it. We have no customers yet. A few honest conversations are worth more than any feature. Keep each to 15 minutes.

## Who to ask (aim for five)

1. A **bug-bounty researcher** who has had a severity downgraded or a payout disputed.
2. A **security lead or founder** at a protocol that runs a bounty.
3. A **support or operations lead** at a business that handles warranty, return or refund claims.
4. A **freelancer or an agency owner** who has fought about whether work was "complete".
5. A **grant or DAO operations person** who has had to explain why an application was rejected.

## How to ask (do not pitch first)

Start with their last real dispute, then show the product only at the end. Do not lead them. If they say "that would be useful", ask when they last needed it and what they did instead.

1. Tell me about the last time you had a disagreement with the other side over whether a claim, report or piece of work qualified. What happened?
2. How did you settle it, how long did it take, and what did it cost in time or money?
3. What was the actual disagreement: the rule itself, or a fact about what happened?
4. Who made the final call? Did both sides accept that person? Did you think it was fair?
5. Did you know the rule before it started? Could it have changed during the dispute?
6. What evidence did each side share, and how was it checked?
7. What would have to be true for you to trust a result that came from published rules plus a neutral reviewer you both accepted?
8. Would you use that for your next dispute? What would stop you?
9. Who would pay for something like this, you or the other side or a platform? Roughly how much would be too much?
10. Who else should I talk to?

11. If the other side would have to create a wallet to take part, would they? What if they could approve with their fingerprint or face, and you submitted it for them and paid the fee?
12. What would you need to trust a customer's passkey as "this is really my customer"? (Their login on your site, a call, a read-back code?)
13. If a customer lost their phone mid-dispute, what would you expect to happen?

After question 13, show the home page's real case and the receipt, and ask: "What is confusing? What is missing?"

## Outreach message

> Hi [name], I'm building a small open prototype for settling disputes under rules that are published in advance, and where only the facts two sides disagree on go to a neutral reviewer. I'm trying to learn whether it matches a real problem, not to sell anything. Could I ask you a few questions about the last time you had a dispute over [a bug severity / a claim / whether work was complete]? 15 minutes, and I will not name you or quote you without your permission.

## Rules for using what you hear

- Quote only with explicit permission, and describe people by role unless they say otherwise.
- Do not paraphrase "that's interesting" into "they would use it". Write down what they actually said.
- One strong objection is worth more than three compliments. Put the objections in the submission as honest limits.
